What each model is allowed to be seen by

A feature has been able to say it is confidential since the catalogue was written. It was free text, it appeared on one screen, and nothing read it — not the model that consumed it, not the document compiled from that model, not one control anywhere. A field that describes a legal obligation and reaches no decision is a field that will be wrong, because nothing ever depends on it being right.

A model's class is derived, never declared. The classes are totally ordered, so propagation is a maximum, and a maximum is the one rule about this nobody can argue with: a thing built out of parts is not less sensitive than its most sensitive part. Declaring a model higher is allowed — an output can be more disclosive than any single input, which is most of what re-identification is — and declaring it lower is refused, naming the feature that forces the floor.

What this instance is holding, and for how long
The invocation log records the shape of every call — who, what for, which version, how long, how it ended — and deliberately holds no content. The inference log is the other half, and it holds content only where somebody decided it should. The digest is the default and the values are the exception: a digest proves what was asked and what was answered without holding either, which is what AI Act Art. 12 traceability actually needs.

Retention runs the other way from what people expect. restricted data is kept for the shortest time, not the longest. The instinct is that important data should be kept longer; the obligation is that data you should not be holding should be held for less time.

And the interesting calls are never sampled out. A refusal, a boundary violation and an error are kept whatever the rate says — the sample exists to make the rare thing visible, and sampling out the rare thing is exactly backwards. Every row says which reason put it there, because a sample nobody can explain is a sample nobody trusts.
How long each thing is kept, and what stops deletion
Retention is per class because the obligations are. AI Act Art. 19 asks for logs over the system's lifetime; SOX asks seven years of what supported a financial statement; data protection asks that personal data be held for less time, not more. A single estate-wide number satisfies whichever of those is loudest and quietly breaks the others.

A WORM option is a claim about where something is stored, not a flag on a row. A directory with the permission bit cleared is an honest limit and not immutable storage, because whoever can clear the bit can set it again. A register that reports compliance because somebody chose WORM from a dropdown is worse than one with no such field — the tick is what stops anybody asking.

A period is a floor, never a ceiling. It says how long something must be kept, not when it must go. Confusing may now be deleted with must now be deleted is how a register loses the record that was about to be asked for.

{{ holds.active }} legal hold(s) are in force, and a hold overrides every period above — the one direction this platform lets a control be overridden. A hold has no end date, and that is correct: it inverts the rule every other bounded thing here follows, because a hold ends when the matter ends and when that is cannot be known when it is placed. Putting a date on it would be guessing at a litigation timetable and calling the guess a control.
Models that were deleted, and what went with them

Deletion is the one act here with no workflow, no reversal and no second signature. A tombstone is what stays: it makes a historical reference resolve to there was one, and here is what happened to it rather than to nothing, and it is why the identifier cannot be handed out again. That last part is the load-bearing one. A URN is derived from the name, so without a marker a second model registered under a deleted one's name would silently inherit its whole evidence history — and every hash would still verify, because the chain would be intact. It would simply be about the wrong model.

What a document inherits
A document is at least as sensitive as the most sensitive model it describes, by the same rule. The surprising answer is the useful one: a board pack compiled across the estate inherits the join of every model in it, which is nearly always higher than whoever asked for it expected. That is not a fault in the arithmetic — it is what aggregation does, and it is exactly why the classification of a summary is worth computing rather than assuming. {% if report.models %} On this estate that comes to {{ documents.classification }}{% if documents.pii %}, and it aggregates personal data{% endif %}. {% endif %}
