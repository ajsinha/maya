What each grant may spend
Three limits, and they are not three sizes of the same thing. The rate bounds calls per minute and protects the downstream system from a loop. The quota bounds calls per window and protects the authorisation from being used more than anybody intended — a grant issued for a nightly batch and exercised forty thousand times a day is being used for something nobody approved, and no rate limit would notice, because none of it is fast. The cost budget is the only one whose unit is not calls, which is exactly why it is the one that matters for a token-metered generative model: ten calls can cost more than ten thousand.

The limit is on the grant, not on the caller. A service account holding four grants should not have one runaway use exhaust the other three — a limit on the principal turns an incident in one product into an outage in three unrelated ones.

A refused call does not count against the quota. Otherwise a caller in a retry loop could never recover: the retries would consume the allowance the retries are waiting for, and the grant would be dead until the window rolled despite never having been used successfully. The refusals are still recorded — that is what makes who is hammering this answerable.

The rate window slides. A fixed one-minute bucket permits twice the limit across a boundary: sixty calls at 11:59:59 and sixty more at 12:00:01. The whole point of a rate limit is the burst, so measuring it in a way that misses the burst measures nothing.
