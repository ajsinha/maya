One number, and the six that made it

Every model risk function is eventually asked for a health score, and every health score is eventually mistrusted. Two things are done differently here, and both are visible on this page rather than described in a methodology note nobody reads.

A mean dilutes, so the band is not the mean

A model with an excellent AUC, no drift and a validation that expired eighteen months ago averages to a comfortable number, and the comfortable number is what goes on the slide. The score is a weighted mean over what could be measured; the band is that mean capped by conditions no amount of good news elsewhere may outweigh. When the two disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

An unmeasured component is not a good one

A model with no monitors, no validation and no findings has nothing bad to say about it, and a naive composite reads that as health. Absent components are excluded from the denominator and named, and coverage travels with the score everywhere it goes: 92 at 30% coverage is not a healthy model, it is a model nobody has looked at.
