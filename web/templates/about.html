ABOUT

What this is, and why it exists

A large bank runs 800–3,000 models and tens of thousands of end-user computations. Almost none of them are what MLOps tooling assumes.

The gap

Governance platforms have excellent workflow but cannot see the artefact: the record asserts that a model was validated, and nothing establishes that the model described is the model executing. ML platforms have excellent artefact lineage but no concept of materiality, approved use, effective challenge or overlay — and never see 60–80% of the estate.

Buying both produces two inventories that disagree, and the disagreement is itself an audit finding.

The definition

A model is a parameter object P, an input X, an output Y, and a kernel f : P ⊗ X → Y. Training is one way of inhabiting P — not part of the definition of a model. A closed-form pricer is the case where P is empty; a vendor black box is the case where it exists but cannot be inspected.

What MAYA does not do

It does not execute models. It manages them and issues hooks — signed, expiring, entitlement-bound execution contracts — which an execution engine acts on. A captive engine ships as a default so a deployment works out of the box, but it is a consumer of the same public contract any external engine uses, and it can be switched off without changing anything else.
