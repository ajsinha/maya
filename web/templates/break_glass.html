Emergency access, and what happened without it

The admin role is described as break-glass and is exempt from the incompatible-roles check. That is not break-glass. It is a standing account that happens to be powerful — and a standing powerful account is precisely the thing break-glass exists to replace. Break-glass is defined by being closed by default: asked for, agreed to by somebody else, ending on its own, and read afterwards by somebody who was not in the incident.

What happened without a grant
You cannot find break-glass abuse by watching break-glass. Anybody misusing emergency access would simply not open a grant for it, so the abuse is never in the break-glass log — it is in what is missing from it. A unilateral grant is allowed and is marked. Dual authorisation means the second person cannot be the first, and at three in the morning there may be only one person awake. Refusing outright is how an institution ends up with a shared password in a safe, which has no name, no reason, no window and no review — so a single-person grant opens, gets a shorter window, and its review is not optional.

Expiry is derived, never a flag somebody clears. A grant is closed to the platform the instant its window ends, whether or not the batch has run — a grant only closed when a batch runs is open whenever the batch is not, which is exactly when it would matter.
