Competitive landscape

Where MAYA sits among the products that touch model governance, and how it does the things the others do not.

About MAYA Help

Three families of product touch the problem MAYA addresses, and each is good at what it does. Model risk management platforms, such as SAS Model Risk Management, IBM OpenPages Model Risk Governance, ModelOp, ValidMind and Yields, hold the inventory, the tiering, the approvals, the independent challenge and the findings. But they see the model through documents about it. ML platforms and model registries, such as MLflow, Databricks, Amazon SageMaker, Google Vertex AI and Azure Machine Learning, hold the artefact, its runs and its deployment. But they have no record of approved use, challenge or remediation. Feature stores, such as Feast, Tecton and Hopsworks, solve point-in-time correctness for the data, and stop at the data.

The pattern is consistent: one family governs the model without seeing it, and the others see it without governing it. MAYA keeps one record that does both, for the whole chain behind a number: the data as it stood, sealed; the mathematics, evaluable; and the licence to fit and to run, granted in advance and refusable.

What the rows have in common
  • It derives what others ask somebody to type: the input contract from the formula, a pin’s identity from its contents, and what a training row could have known from two clocks.
  • It refuses before the fact. A warrant is a licence, not a log entry, so an unapproved fit or an unlicensed run is stopped rather than recorded.
  • Its evidence leaves with a verifier. A bundle is checked on a machine with no MAYA on it.
The practical reading

MAYA is a complement, not a replacement. It licenses what an ML platform trains and serves, and supplies the evidence a model risk platform records.
